Retell Parity Scorecard

Where CallDeskTech stands against Retell AI, feature by feature, plus the real quality numbers from today's live shopper calls — used to decide what to build next.

Compiled 2026-09-21, updated five times same day — 16 gaps closed across three rounds, 2 real security bugs fixed, 31 new languages added (11 → 42), and a second TTS backend wired in · Retell inventory from public docs/changelog/pricing (see caveats) · Our side from the live codebase and real production calls placed today

Round 5 update — answered "how does Retell reach 55 languages if they also use ElevenLabs?" for real: they don't rely on one provider — 6+ TTS providers, union of coverage. So we wired Cartesia (sonic-3.6, 44 languages) as a real second per-language TTS backend, same shape as Retell's approach. 13 more languages added (languages Cartesia covers that ElevenLabs doesn't, with real Deepgram STT codes): Hungarian, Norwegian, Vietnamese, Bengali, Thai, Georgian, Telugu, Gujarati, Kannada, Marathi, Punjabi, Urdu, Hebrew — 29 → 42 built. Separately, the 13 languages left unverified after Round 4 (Korean, Bulgarian, Croatian, Czech, Danish, Filipino, Finnish, Malay, Romanian, Slovak, Swedish, Tamil, Ukrainian) were all real-call-verified this round too. A real bug was found and fixed during Cartesia verification: the first version's language→backend switch silently never fired (a guard meant to protect an explicit tenant choice was defeated by the call context always setting that field), causing a 404 on the very first real Hungarian test call. Fixed, redeployed, and 5 of the 13 new languages re-verified clean (Hungarian, Vietnamese, Bengali, Georgian, Hebrew) plus a Spanish regression check to confirm the fix didn't disturb the existing 29.

Team & enterprise

HIPAA / SOC 2 / GDPR compliancePartial
Real groundwork shipped today: an audit log (`calldesk_audit_log`, wired into 6 sensitive routes) plus draft subprocessors + readiness gap-analysis docs, both explicitly headed "requires legal/compliance review before any external claim." Both real gaps the analysis found are now fixed and live-verified: API-key revocation now requires owner/admin (was any active member), and per-call deletion (`DELETE /api/calls/[id]`, owner/admin, audit-logged) now backs the Privacy page's claim — deployed and confirmed against production with a real API-key test (revoke correctly 403s, delete correctly removes the row and audit-logs it). One honest scope limit remains: call deletion doesn't yet synchronously purge the underlying Twilio-hosted recording audio, since calldesktech holds no Twilio credentials of its own — that's call-loop-poc's existing retention sweep. Still no certification, no BAA, no legal review — realistic path is legal review of the drafts, BAAs with Anthropic/Twilio/Deepgram, an incident-response runbook, then a paid SOC2 Type I audit (~2-3 months) and Type II (another 3-6 months of observation). Do not represent this row as "compliant" externally.
