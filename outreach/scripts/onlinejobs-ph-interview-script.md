# Phone-outreach hire — interview script (OnlineJobs.ph candidates)

Goal: find out, in ~30 minutes, whether this person can call a US small-business
owner cold, sound natural, stay honest, and take a "no" gracefully. Not a general
skills interview — score only what's below.

Bring: this script, a notepad, the scoresheet at the bottom.

## 1. Warm-up (5 min) — just talk, don't score yet

Ask, conversationally:
- "Walk me through a cold-calling job you've done before. What were you selling
  or asking for?"
- "What's the worst call you ever got, and what did you do?"
- "Have you ever called US small businesses specifically? Any accent/timezone
  issues come up?"

What you're listening for here: do they talk about calls in terms of a script
they followed robotically, or in terms of reading the other person? The second
is what we want. Note it, move on — this section doesn't get a score.

## 2. Explain the real job (2 min) — say this almost verbatim

> "You'd be calling small US businesses — freight brokers, insurance agencies,
> home service companies — that we already emailed. Most already got an email
> from us offering a free trial of an AI phone agent that answers their calls
> when they're busy or after hours. Your job on the call is NOT to sell. It's
> to ask one honest question — does your business ever miss calls? — and if
> they're interested, get them to say yes to forwarding their overflow calls to
> a number we give them. That's the whole ask. You will never invent a
> statistic, never claim results we haven't proven, and never pretend to be
> someone you're not. If they say no, you say thanks and hang up. No pressure,
> no arguing."

Then ask: "Does that make sense? Any questions before we role-play it?"
Answer their questions honestly. If they ask "what if they ask about pricing"
— tell them: "It's free for a 2-week trial, capped at 50 minutes of calls, no
credit card. That's the only number you're allowed to give them."

## 3. Role-play round 1 — receptive owner (score this)

Say: "I'm going to play a business owner. You call me. Go."

Then play this character:

> You are Dave, owner of Ridgeline Plumbing, a 6-person shop in Austin, TX.
> You're a little distracted (mid-job) but not rude. If the caller asks a good
> question, engage honestly: yes, calls do go to voicemail sometimes when the
> crew's on a job. You're mildly interested but not eager. If they ask you to
> forward calls, ask "what number do I forward to" — see if they have an
> answer ready (they may not — that's fine, note it, don't penalize hard for a
> job-specific detail like that).

Let it run 2-3 minutes. Don't help them.

## 4. Role-play round 2 — hostile/skeptical owner (score this)

Say: "Same thing, different business, I'll play someone less friendly."

> You're Priya, running a small insurance agency. You've had three cold calls
> today already. Open with: "Is this a sales call?" — flat, annoyed. If they
> push past a polite no, get firmer: "I said I'm not interested, please take
> me off your list." If they handle it well (acknowledge, thank you, hang up
> cleanly), soften at the very end and say "actually wait, what is this about"
> — see if they recover naturally without gloating or re-pitching aggressively.

## 5. Score each round (1–5) right after it happens, not at the end

| | Round 1 | Round 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Opens with a question, not a pitch | | |
| Sounds like a real conversation, not read-aloud | | |
| Handles pushback/rejection without pressure | | |
| States only what we actually offer — no invented stats, no fake urgency | | |
| Asks for the concrete next step (forward-number), not vague "can I follow up" | | |
| Clarity / accent intelligibility to a US ear | | |
| Recovers gracefully if the tone shifts mid-call (round 2 only) | | — |

**Hard no, regardless of other scores:** anything below a 3 on "no invented
stats" or "handles pushback without pressure." We already had a real incident
of a caller/script inventing statistics and a fake persona under our name —
this is non-negotiable, not a nice-to-have.

## 6. Closing questions (2 min)

- "If a business owner asks you something you don't know the answer to, what
  do you do?" — right answer: say you'll have someone follow up by email, never
  guess or invent an answer.
- "How many of these calls do you think you could do in an hour, realistically,
  leaving room for voicemails and no-answers?" — sanity-check their expectation
  against reality (most calls will be voicemail or a quick no).
